# IoT-Based Smart Urban Heat Island Monitoring and Mitigation System

## Overview
This project reviews research on **Urban Heat Islands (UHI)** and explores how an IoT-based system could monitor urban heat and support practical mitigation.

## Background
- **Urban Heat Island (UHI):** Urban areas can become warmer than nearby rural areas because of roads and buildings that absorb and retain heat, limited vegetation, and human activity.
- **Global warming:** The long-term rise in Earth's average temperature. It can increase the baseline temperature on which local urban heat builds.
- **El Niño:** A natural climate pattern involving unusually warm sea-surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific. It can influence temperatures and weather patterns around the world; its effects vary by region and event.

## What We Are Comparing
Across the reviewed papers, we compare:
- Study location, research objective, and data sources.
- Methods such as satellite remote sensing, land-surface-temperature mapping, ground measurements, modelling, and IoT/AI forecasting.
- Main findings, identified heat hotspots, and reported limitations.
- Mitigation approaches, including vegetation, reflective/cool materials, and urban-design changes.

## Why This Matters
Many studies map heat using satellite images or analyse selected locations and time periods. Our proposed direction is to explore how **continuous IoT sensor readings**, combined with spatial heat maps and local analysis, could complement these approaches and help identify when and where mitigation may be needed.

## Note
UHI, global warming, and El Niño are related but distinct: UHI is a local urban effect, global warming is a long-term global trend, and El Niño is a naturally recurring climate pattern. Their impacts should be analysed separately before drawing conclusions about how they interact.
